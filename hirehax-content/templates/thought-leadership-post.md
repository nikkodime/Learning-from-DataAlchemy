# Template: Thought leadership post (Search / Org & Leadership Design / Future-Ready)

Used for pillars 2, 3, and 4 — the medium-length, Wednesday/Friday posts that carry the actual point of view.

## Structure

1. **The point, first.** One or two sentences stating the position. Not a question, not a story opener. Per the voice rules: lead with the point.
2. **The consequence, stated as logic.** Why this is true, in mechanism terms, not fear terms. This is where the "insider" specificity goes: what actually happens inside a biotech/diagnostics org when this dynamic plays out.
3. **The contrarian edge.** One sentence that pushes against the comfortable, consensus version of this topic. Per the voice rules, "a point of view is the product."
4. **The close.** Either a sharpened restatement of the opening point, or a direct question that a CEO/board member would actually sit with (not a generic "thoughts?").

## Length

100-180 words. Long enough to carry an argument, short enough to be read on a phone in a board meeting's dead time.

## Skeleton

```
[Position, stated flatly, one or two sentences.]

[Mechanism: what actually happens, described plainly, one idea per sentence.]

[Contrarian edge: the thing most people in this conversation won't say.]

[Close: restated point, or a real question.]
```

## Worked example (Pillar 2 — Search as Architecture)

```
Most executive searches fail before the first candidate call. They fail in the week the mandate gets written, when the company describes the role it had eighteen months ago instead of the one it needs eighteen months from now.

A search brief built from the current org chart hires a replacement. A search brief built from where the company is going hires a leader who is still right in two years.

The uncomfortable part: most boards don't actually disagree on the destination. They've just never written it down before opening the search, so the recruiter is left reverse-engineering intent from a job description.

Before the next mandate goes out, the useful exercise isn't the candidate profile. It's a page describing the company at month twenty-four, and hiring backward from there.
```

## Worked example (Pillar 4 — Future-Ready / AI)

```
AI is not removing roles from the org chart. It is removing the layers between decision and evidence, which is a different problem and most leadership teams are solving the wrong one.

A CMO whose value was synthesizing data for the CEO has less to do when the CEO can query the data directly. A CMO whose value is judgment about what the data means still has everything to do.

Boards are asking "which roles does AI replace." The sharper question is which roles were only ever justified by information scarcity, because that scarcity is the thing actually disappearing.

Worth an honest hour with the executive team: which of your current roles exist to move information, and which exist to make a call once the information has already arrived.
```
