# Template: Operating Principle / Founder POV post (Pillar 5)

Unpacks one of the four operating principles from the deck, or recounts a specific, named-context (company can stay unnamed, era/role should be concrete) moment from Amgen, Vertex, or Menarini that earns the "we operate like insiders" claim.

The four principles, one per post, rotated:
1. We start with the end.
2. We think in systems, not seats.
3. We operate like insiders.
4. We tell the truth fast.

## Structure

1. **State the principle** in the deck's own words or close to it.
2. **Ground it in one specific, real moment.** Not "in my experience, this happens often." One scene: a room, a decision, a consequence. This is the pillar where first-person "I" is allowed and expected.
3. **Extract the generalizable lesson**, stated as something the reader can act on this week.

## Length

120-200 words. This pillar can run slightly longer than Signal or thought-leadership posts because it's carrying a story, not just an argument.

## Skeleton

```
[Principle, stated.]

[Specific scene: what happened, where, what the stakes were. First person where it's genuinely first-person experience.]

[What it revealed, generalized into a lesson the reader can use.]
```

## Worked example (Principle: "We tell the truth fast")

```
We tell the truth fast. It sounds like a value statement. It is actually a scheduling decision.

Years ago, inside a diagnostics business mid-launch, I sat in a room where everyone already knew the commercial leader wasn't going to make the transition to the next stage. Nobody said it for eleven weeks. The org kept operating around the gap: decisions routed sideways, the team below him managed up instead of out, momentum leaked quietly the entire time.

The truth didn't get any easier to say in week eleven than it would have been in week one. It just got more expensive. The honest conversation is never free. It's only ever a question of which price you pay: the cost of saying it now, or the compounding cost of everyone around you managing the fact that you haven't.
```
